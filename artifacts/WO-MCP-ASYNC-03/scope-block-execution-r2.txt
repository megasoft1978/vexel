Required item 3 and AC-5 require legacy notifications/progress and in-flight
notifications/cancelled to operate during native GIF export, with no eventual
response after cancellation. The product's public transport is the stdio
sidecar, not a client connection directly to the app socket (docs/mcp.md:3-6).

Source evidence, read only:
- app/vexel_mcp_main.c:119 and :131: the stdin loop calls relay_line synchronously
  after a complete request; it cannot read another stdin message until that call
  returns.
- app/vexel_mcp_main.c:96-106: relay_line sends the request, then waits for exactly
  one socket line through read_response_line for a request with an ID. It does
  not match response IDs or distinguish server notifications from responses.
- app/vexel_mcp_main.c:50-63: recv may consume multiple lines, but only bytes
  before the first newline are appended; the rest of that recv chunk are lost.
- app/vexel_mcp_main.c:90-92: socket waits have a 30-second timeout.

Consequences (source-derived, not a live-client or runtime-test claim):
1. With no progressToken, the original legacy export leaves relay_line waiting;
   a queued client cancellation cannot be read/forwarded while it waits.
2. With progressToken, the first progress notification ends that wait as though
   it were the response. Subsequent notifications/final results are not drained
   while stdin is idle and may be returned by a later request's relay_line.
   Coalesced notification/result lines can be silently discarded.
3. A canceled original request correctly producing no response does not fit
   this one-request/one-line transport contract. Correct broker-side behavior
   alone cannot make stdio forwarding reliable.
